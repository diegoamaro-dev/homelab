# Voice Lab — Round 1 (native TTS casting) — record

- Date: 2026-07-27
- Type: documentation record (repository-external work; no repo code or artifacts committed)
- Phase relation: F-6 (Voice Quality) — exploratory casting, **not** a production change

## Scope

Aurora's production voice today is **Piper**. This record captures the outcome of **Round 1** of
an isolated, **repository-external Voice Lab** used to cast a candidate **native** TTS voice for
Aurora. The Voice Lab itself — its code, model weights, container images and audio outputs —
lives **outside this repository and is intentionally not committed** (evaluation tooling, not
production infrastructure).

## Method (blind, casting-first)

- A **fixed Spanish evaluation corpus** (short samples spanning greeting, long-form paragraph,
  question, affirmation, emotional line, technical explanation, numbers/dates, abbreviations,
  proper names, punctuation/pauses) was synthesized by **every** engine under **identical
  conditions**.
- Every output was **loudness-normalized** (EBU R128) so perceived volume could not bias the
  result, then presented **blind**: each engine relabeled **Voice A/B/…**, order **randomized**,
  the engine↔label mapping **sealed** and revealed only **after** scores were submitted.
- **Subjective casting was the primary axis** (naturalness, Castilian accent, warmth, listening
  comfort, emotional consistency, daily usability, overall preference). Engineering metrics
  (relative speed, resource use) were **tie-breakers only**. STT round-trip (intelligibility)
  tooling was left dormant for Round 1.

## Engines cast (native, female Spanish)

- **Piper** — incumbent production voice (MIT).
- **Kokoro** (`ef_dora`) — Apache-2.0.
- **XTTS v2** — Coqui CPML (non-commercial): included as a quality reference, **not** a
  shippable candidate.
- **Chatterbox** — MIT.
- A hosted engine was included **reference-only** (generated by hand and imported — **no cloud
  call from AMAROLAB, no credentials stored**).

## Result & decision

- **Kokoro `ef_dora` was the preferred voice at ~70% confidence, ahead of the incumbent Piper.**
- **Kokoro is now the native TTS reference candidate.**
- **Piper remains the production voice. No production migration occurred** (Home Assistant,
  Open WebUI and the Piper services are unchanged).
- A focused Kokoro audit found it strong, permissively licensed and fast, but with a **limited
  prosody/accent control surface** (only speaking-rate and punctuation) — it cannot be tuned
  toward a stronger Castilian accent, more warmth, natural breathing, or conversational pacing.

## Round 2 (voice cloning) — designed, NOT started

- Round 2 is **architecturally designed**: evaluate whether a **legally-permitted Castilian**
  reference voice, cloned by a **local** engine, beats the Kokoro baseline while preserving the
  local-first trust boundary. Permissively-licensed local cloners are the production-relevant
  candidates; non-commercially-licensed engines stay reference-only. No cloud cloning services.
- **No cloning implementation, engine build or model download has been started.**
- **Next gate: define Aurora's synthetic voice identity** (a product / long-term-identity
  decision) — then record a legally-permitted reference clip (with consent + provenance) and run
  Round 2 under the same blind method, one engine at a time with a checkpoint after each.
